# Voice fingerprint

**Typical length**: 900-1,400 characters. Range 870-1,420 across the five samples.

**Sentence rhythm**: Wide variation. Short flat declaratives ("It does not prove
that.") set against long clauses that run on while the thought completes ("The
thing that actually matters is what the carriers do with the blank sailings
programme in October."). Longest sentence is roughly four times the shortest.

**Paragraph shape**: Real paragraphs, 2-4 sentences, blank line between. Never
single-sentence lines stacked as beats. Occasionally a short final paragraph.

**Opening move**: Opens with a hard fact or number, no preamble. "Spot rates on
Asia-North Europe dropped 14% in a fortnight and..." / "The EUDR guidance
published last Thursday has a detail..." / "Antwerp-Bruges handled 3.4m TEU in Q1,
up 4.6% year on year..." Never a question, never a scene-setter.

**Closing move**: Flat and practical, no call to action. "...you are budgeting on a
number that has a shelf life of about five weeks." / "Worth checking yours before
you renew. Anyway, back to it." One post asks a real question ("If someone has had
a straight answer from their customs authority I would genuinely like to hear it")
but it is a specific request, not engagement bait.

**Person and stance**: First person singular, used sparingly and only for things
actually done ("I have read it twice", "Spent Tuesday in a workshop"). Makes firm
claims. Names companies, ports, indices and regulations directly. Corrects other
people's readings of the news.

**Formality and jargon**: Trade jargon used unglossed - TEU, blank sailings,
demurrage, bonded warehousing, breakbulk, EUDR. Contractions almost never appear:
writes "does not", "I have", "it is". British spelling and idiom throughout:
"fortnight", "programme", "sceptical", "eighteen months".

**Furniture**: No emoji. No hashtags. No bullet points or lists. No ALL CAPS. Plain
paragraphs only.

**Recurring phrases and constructions**:
- "Anyway." or "Anyway," as a pivot into the closing thought (posts 1 and 5)
- "The thing that actually matters is..."
- Numbers written with units and periods intact: "3.4m TEU", "14%", "7 days to 5"
- A stated belief followed by an immediate correction of it: "the announcement
  frames it as recovery. Reading the same release..."
- Willingness to say what is not known: "I am still not completely certain"

**English patterns to preserve**: British spelling and vocabulary. No contractions.

**Things they never do**: No emoji, no hashtags, no lists, no "Here's the thing",
no closing CTA, no motivational framing, no three-item parallel structure, never
opens with a question.
