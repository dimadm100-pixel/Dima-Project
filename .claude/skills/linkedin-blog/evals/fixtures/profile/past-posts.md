## Post 1 - 2026-06-12

Spot rates on Asia-North Europe dropped 14% in a fortnight and I have had three
people tell me this proves the capacity crunch is over. It does not prove that.
It proves August was overbooked and September is not, which is a different
sentence entirely.

The thing that actually matters is what the carriers do with the blank sailings
programme in October. Last year they pulled 11% of scheduled capacity in six
weeks and the rate floor held. If they do it again the drop reverses before most
planning cycles have finished reacting to it.

Anyway. If your budget for next year assumes today's spot rate, you are budgeting
on a number that has a shelf life of about five weeks.

## Post 2 - 2026-05-28

The EUDR guidance published last Thursday has a detail that I think a lot of
people are going to miss. The due diligence statement requirement applies at the
point the goods are placed on the market, not at import. For anyone running
consignment stock or bonded warehousing that is a meaningfully different date,
and in a few cases a different legal entity carrying the obligation.

I have read it twice and I am still not completely certain how it applies to
goods already in a bonded warehouse before the deadline. If someone has had a
straight answer from their customs authority I would genuinely like to hear it,
because ours has been vague for a month.

## Post 3 - 2026-05-14

Spent Tuesday in a workshop where a WMS vendor demonstrated real-time inventory
visibility across 14 sites. Genuinely impressive product. Then someone from the
floor asked what the system does when the count is wrong, and the answer was that
it flags a discrepancy for review.

That is the whole problem in one exchange. Nobody in that room lacked visibility
of their stock discrepancies. They had spreadsheets full of them. What they
lacked was anyone with the authority to decide what to do about a discrepancy
before it turned into a stockout.

We keep buying instruments when what we need is a decision-maker.

## Post 4 - 2026-04-30

Antwerp-Bruges handled 3.4m TEU in Q1, up 4.6% year on year, and the announcement
frames it as recovery. Reading the same release, container throughput is up but
conventional and breakbulk are down again, which is the fourth quarter in a row
that has happened.

The recovery is real if you are in containers. If you are moving project cargo or
steel it has been a poor eighteen months and nothing in this release suggests
that has turned. Two different ports sharing a name.

## Post 5 - 2026-04-16

A quiet week, so a small observation instead of news.

Every carrier contract I have looked at this year has moved detention and demurrage
free time from 7 days to 5. Every single one. Nobody announced it, there was no
press release, it just appeared in the terms. On a 200 container a month operation
that is not a rounding error, it is roughly a full-time person's salary in
avoidable charges if your gate-out process is slow.

Worth checking yours before you renew. Anyway, back to it.
