# Audience

**Who follows the user / who they want to reach**
- Supply chain and logistics managers at mid-size European manufacturers and retailers, 50-1000 employees
- A smaller group of 3PL account managers and freight forwarders
- Some procurement leads who inherited logistics

**What those people do all day**
- Chase late containers, argue with carriers about rates, explain delays to sales teams
- Run their planning in ERP plus a lot of spreadsheets
- Have no time and read LinkedIn between meetings

**What the user wants them to think after reading**
- That Maya reads the primary sources they don't have time for, and tells them what actually changes for their week

# Beats

1. Ocean and air freight rates, capacity, carrier alliances
2. Customs, trade rules, tariffs, EU regulation (CBAM, EUDR, customs reform)
3. Port and rail disruption, labour disputes, canal and chokepoint issues
4. Warehouse automation and WMS/TMS software, where it actually works
5. Inventory practice - what companies are doing with safety stock and nearshoring

**Adjacent topics** worth using in a thin news week:
- Manufacturing PMI and freight demand data
- Insurance and war-risk premiums on shipping routes

**Out of scope**
- Personal productivity, leadership advice, hiring takes, anything motivational

# Sources

- Lloyd's List, Journal of Commerce, The Loadstar, Splash247
- Drewry and Freightos rate indices
- European Commission trade and customs pages, national customs authorities
- Carrier investor updates - Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd, CMA CGM, MSC announcements
- Port authority notices - Rotterdam, Antwerp-Bruges, Hamburg, Felixstowe

# Recurring angles

- Rate volatility is a planning problem, not a procurement problem
- Most logistics software is sold on visibility and bought by people who needed decisions
- Regulation lands on the people with the least warning, usually a customs desk
- Sceptical of nearshoring announcements until volumes show up in the data
