# Shaping the post

## Length

`profile/voice-profile.md` records the length the user actually writes at, and that figure governs. The numbers here are the fallback when it is silent.

Default target **1,300 to 2,100 characters**, roughly 200 to 320 words. That is enough for three items with a real thought attached to each, plus an opening and a close, and short enough to finish on a phone.

Under 900 characters, three items cannot get more than a headline each and the post reads as a list. Over 2,400 the drop-off is steep. LinkedIn's hard limit is 3,000 characters; there is no reason to go near it.

Let the count follow the material. A week with one genuinely significant story and two smaller ones should give the big one most of the space.

## The first 200 characters

LinkedIn truncates at roughly 200 characters with a "see more" link. Everything after that is only read by people the opening earned. So the opening has to carry actual information or an actual claim, not throat-clearing.

Openings that work: the most surprising fact of the week, stated flat. A claim about what the week showed. A number without its context yet. The one thing that changed for the reader's job.

Openings that don't: "This week has been fascinating in the world of..." / "Three things caught my eye this week." / any rhetorical question. They spend the only guaranteed real estate on nothing.

Match the user's own opening habit from the voice profile before applying any of this - if they consistently open a particular way and it works for them, that beats generic advice.

## Structure

The three items need connective tissue, not numbering. A mini-blog reads as one continuous thought; a bulleted list reads as a newsletter someone automated.

Workable shapes:

- **The argument**: state the claim, then the three items as evidence, then what follows from it.
- **The pattern**: two items that look unrelated, the third that reveals what they share.
- **The correction**: a belief the audience holds, then what this week's evidence does to it.
- **The report**: three things worth knowing, ordered by consequence, with the writer's read on each.

Whatever the shape, each item should carry its concrete detail and the user's take on it. An item without a take is a headline.

If the user's samples show they use numbered or bulleted lists and their audience responds to that, follow the samples. This section describes defaults, not rules.

## Formatting

LinkedIn renders no markdown. Asterisks, pound signs and underscores appear literally, which looks broken. Write plain text. Emphasis comes from line breaks, sentence length and word choice.

Line breaks are the only formatting available and they carry real weight. Blank lines between short paragraphs make a post feel airy and quick; dense paragraphs feel more considered. Which one is right depends entirely on the user's samples.

## Closing

The close should land the point, not summarise it. A flat statement of what the writer thinks happens next, an admission of what they can't tell yet, or a genuine question they'd want answered all work.

Avoid: recapping the three items, "what do you think?", a call to follow or connect, and a closing line that could be appended to any post on any topic.

## Sources and links

Keep URLs out of the post body. Posts with outbound links reach fewer people, and a wall of URLs looks like aggregation. List sources under the post in the delivery block so the user has them, and note when it's worth dropping them into the first comment - that gets the citation without the reach penalty.

Naming the outlet inside the post ("per the FT," "in their Q3 filing") is different, and worth doing. It costs nothing and it signals the writer read something.

## Hashtags and tagging

Two or three hashtags maximum, and only if the samples use them. Many strong finance accounts use none at all; if the samples have none, adding them is a visible change in voice. Tagging a company or person mentioned in an item can help reach, but only when the mention is positive or neutral - never tag someone into criticism on their behalf. Flag any tagging suggestion in the "Before you post" note and let the user decide.
