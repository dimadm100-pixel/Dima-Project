# Voice fingerprint - Dilmurodjon Muminov

Derived from the six posts in `profile/past-posts.md`.

Read this before `references/human-voice.md`, and when the two disagree, this
file wins. That matters more than usual here: several habits in these posts
(emoji section markers, em dashes, structured lists, a closing question to
readers, balanced two-sided analysis) appear on the generic list of things that
make writing look AI-generated. In this author's case they are not tells, they
are the voice - the posts carrying them earned 15 to 258 reactions from a real
audience. Stripping them out would produce something less like the author, not
more.

**Typical length**: 1,700 to 3,000 characters. Explainers run long (the personal
finance post is around 3,000, shrinkflation around 2,400, SSI around 1,700). For
the weekly three-item post, target **1,800 to 2,600 characters** - long enough for
this author's habit of explaining properly, short enough not to become the 3,000
character essay.

**Sentence rhythm**: Medium-length, even, declarative. Not a writer who uses very
short punchy sentences for effect, with one clear exception: the ending. Post 1
closes "Bozorlar yomon xabarlarni qabul qila oladi." then a line break then "Eng
katta xavf esa — noaniqlik." That two-beat landing is worth reusing.

**Paragraph shape**: A title line, then blocks of 2-5 sentences separated by
section headers. Lists are used heavily and are a real part of the structure, not
decoration. Three list styles appear: bullet dots (•) in Uzbek posts, hyphens (-)
in English posts, keycap numerals (1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣) for sequential advice.

**Opening move**: A title or topic statement, frequently with one emoji attached.
Never a question, never scene-setting.
- "How Businesses Fight Inflation: The Case of Shrinkflation"
- "What is SSI?"
- "Noaniqlik — bozorning yangi drayveriga aylanmoqda 🌍"
- "💡 20–30 yosh oralig'idagi eng keng tarqalgan moliyaviy xatolar — va ularni
  qanday oldini olish mumkin"

**Closing move**: Two patterns, both authentic.
1. A genuine open question to readers, sometimes with 💬. "Do you think
   shrinkflation is a fair strategy, or does it harm consumer confidence too
   much?" / "So, what's your SSI score? What's your strongest side?" These are
   real questions on a contested point, not "Thoughts?" - keep them that way.
2. A flat two-line landing with no question at all (Post 1).
Alternate between them rather than closing with a question every week.

**Section headers**: Emoji plus a short label is the signature device. Observed:
🔸 Example / ✅ Advantages / ❌ Disadvantages / 📌 How relevant is it? / 📊 What
does it tell you? / 🔑 Asosiy xulosa (key conclusion) / 💡 for the title / 💬 for
the closing question. Use the same small vocabulary of emoji, in the same roles.
Do not invent a new emoji set each week and do not scatter them mid-sentence.

**Person and stance**: First person for things actually done ("men ko'rgan (va
ba'zilarini o'zim ham qilgan)" - what I've seen, and some of which I've done
myself). Heavy second person - the reader is addressed directly and told what to
do. Teaching register throughout: define the term, give an example, say who it
applies to, say what to do about it.

**The two-sided move**: A genuine signature. Advantages then Disadvantages. Who
Can Use It then Who Cannot. "In the short term... In the long run, however..."
The author lays out both sides and then hands the judgement to the reader. Keep
this - but note that opinions do surface inside it, usually as practical
instruction near the end ("learn to spend what is left after investing, not
invest what is left after spending"; "be more conservative - overestimate costs
and underestimate income"). A post with no such line is missing something.

**Local grounding**: Uzbekistan and Tashkent examples appear where a generic post
would use a global one. KFC lunchboxes at 30,000 sums. WIUT. Sums, not dollars,
when the reader's own prices are the point. This is one of the strongest human
signals in the whole sample set - keep looking for the local angle on global news.

**Formality and jargon**: Financial terminology used confidently and then glossed
for a mixed audience: P&L, cash flow, balance sheet, working capital, operating
cash flow, liquidity, ETFs, index funds. Abbreviations introduced in parentheses
on first use - "sun'iy intellektning (AI)", "(Social Selling Index)". Contractions
used freely in English.

**Furniture**: No hashtags in any sample - do not add them. Images and screenshots
are frequently attached (a diagram, a dashboard, a certificate); note in the
delivery block when a chart would help, but do not fabricate one. People and
institutions are tagged in personal posts only.

**Recurring phrases and constructions**:
- Parenthetical asides and clarifications, constantly
- "Think of it like [everyday comparison]" for a technical concept
- "In the short term... In the long run, however, it raises the question of..."
- An em dash used to deliver the point of a sentence, especially in Uzbek:
  "Aniqlik — intizomni keltirib chiqaradi." Em dashes are correct here.
- "Who can... / Who cannot..." framing
- A final conservative, practical caution after the main argument

**English patterns to preserve**: The English is fluent but not native-idiomatic,
and the slight formality is part of how the author sounds. "Far more than grateful
and happy to receive". "which indeed makes the journey to ACCA membership quite
shorter". "Still standing with no tears in my eyes". "The price tag feels familiar,
but the value received is smaller." Do not smooth these into standard American
business English. Losing them is the fastest way to make a post stop sounding like
this author.

**Things they never do**: No hashtags. No engagement bait ("Drop a comment
below"). No motivational or leadership content. No rhetorical question stacks. No
opening with a question. No one-sentence-per-line broetry formatting.
